# ISS-0017 — Dragging a second canvas card continues moving the previously active card

GitHub: [#17 — Dragging a second canvas card continues moving the previously active card](https://github.com/digimata/terrazzo/issues/17)

## Summary

After dragging one canvas card, attempting to drag a different card can continue moving the previously active card. The user must first click an empty part of the canvas before the second card can be dragged normally.

Severity: degraded. The canvas remains usable, but its primary arrangement interaction becomes unpredictable.

## Reproduction

1. Open a workspace containing at least two Markdown note cards.
2. Drag card A to a new position.
3. Without clicking the empty canvas, press and drag card B.
4. Observe which card moves.

The behavior was observed with Markdown notes. It may affect other filesystem-backed card types.

## Expected behavior

Pressing and dragging card B must immediately make B the drag target and move B only. Card A may remain selected after its first drag, but its selection must not capture a later pointer gesture that begins on B.

## Actual behavior

The drag gesture beginning on card B can continue moving card A. Clicking the empty canvas to clear the active selection restores normal dragging.

## What is working

- The first card can be dragged.
- The second card can be dragged after clearing the active selection.
- The problem appears to concern gesture targeting or selection state rather than layout persistence.

## Hypothesis

This is unconfirmed. Custom card pointer handling and tldraw's selected-shape drag state may disagree about the target of the second pointer-down event. The investigation should also check whether click-to-open behavior or nested card DOM elements prevent tldraw from receiving the new target.

## Acceptance criteria

- After moving card A, pressing and dragging card B moves B only without an intermediate click.
- The behavior works for notes, folders, images, and videos.
- Single-click activation and click-drag movement remain distinguishable.
- Multi-selection behavior remains intentional and documented.
- A regression test covers consecutive drags across two different cards.
